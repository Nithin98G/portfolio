My Journey

Where It All Began

When I came to Canada as a student, my first job was as a Machine Operator at Inject Tech Plastics, an injection molding manufacturing company.

I was never the type of person who only wanted to do the task I was assigned. I constantly wanted to understand why things were done the way they were and what was happening behind the process.

I would stop my supervisors, plant manager, and even the CEO to ask questions about the business, the processes, the numbers, and how everything connected.

One day, I noticed something that didn’t make sense to me. Every day, the same shift report was being deleted and recreated from scratch. It was repetitive work, created a lot of unnecessary effort, and because the information wasn’t being carried forward properly, it was difficult to see trends over time.

So I decided to do something about it.

I went home and, without being asked, built a new version of the report that could make the information easier to track and actually show what was happening over time.

I didn’t tell anyone I was working on it. One day, I brought it in and showed it to them. They were impressed.

That was the first time I really saw how much value I could create by simply looking at a problem differently.

From there, I started finding more ways to make people’s jobs easier — automating repetitive tasks, improving visibility, removing unnecessary steps, and finding waste in processes.

What started as curiosity eventually became something much bigger. That’s when I realized Continuous Improvement was the path I wanted to pursue.

It wasn’t something I chose because of a job title. It came from seeing a problem, understanding why it existed, and wanting to make it better.

That mindset has stayed with me throughout my career. I don’t just want to operate a process. I want to understand it, challenge it, improve it, and find better ways to make it work.
